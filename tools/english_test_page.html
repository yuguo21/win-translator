The Quiet Art of Reading Slowly

Most people believe that speed is the measure of a good reader. In practice, the opposite is often true: a careful reader notices the small hinges on which a story turns.

When you slow down, sentences stop being information and start becoming architecture. You begin to hear the rhythm the author intended, and the meaning arrives with it.

Three habits worth keeping

First, read a paragraph twice before deciding what it means. The second pass costs ten seconds and saves ten minutes of confusion.

Second, keep a pencil nearby. Marking a single word is enough to turn passive reading into a conversation.

Third, stop at the end of each section and say aloud what happened. If you cannot, you have not finished reading it.

A short warning

Slowness is not the same as laziness. The goal is attention, not duration. A focused half hour beats an distracted afternoon every time.

Try it tonight with something short: a single page, a single poem, a single letter you have been meaning to answer.
